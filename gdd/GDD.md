In Fowl Play, one friend signs what he thinks is a free wings-for-life deal at a chicken-themed casino — but the contract's fine print binds anyone who eats the wings too, so sharing them with his three friends drags them all in without any of them signing a thing. Now bound to The House's endless "loyalty" contract, the four of them must gamble their way to enough winnings to void the cursed contract before The House claims them for good.

| Element | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Players | 4 players, local co-op (not competitive) — everyone works toward the same shared goal, though individual choices (greedy bets, risky pushes) can work against the group even without anyone intending to "win" against a teammate. |
| Objectives | Win condition: survive the full run (meet each day's quota, across a set number of days) without failing quota twice, ultimately voiding the contract. Immediate objective each day: hit that day's quota before the 5-minute timer runs out. |
| Rules | The wallet is shared — all winnings and losses apply to one pool, not individual accounts. Only one player may occupy a minigame table at a time. Any player may hit any other player at any time, with no cooldown or restriction — but every hit shakes a small amount of money loose from the target, drawn from the shared wallet, so hitting always carries a collective cost even though it's freely repeatable. A stunned player remains down and cannot act, and can only wake up once they are dropped or thrown — not while being carried. To end the day, all 4 players must be standing together inside a marked exit zone at the same time; reaching it is the only way to manually close out a day. |
| Procedures | Each day in order: (1) The House announces the day's quota and reveals which minigames are open and who is Watched (Blessed/Cursed); (2) Players freely move around the room, choosing which table to approach; (3) At a table, a player places a bet and is given a press-your-luck decision (cash out or push further); (4) At any point, other players may hit a teammate to stun them (small wallet cost per hit), and a stunned player can be picked up and carried — while carried, they cannot wake or act until dropped or thrown; (5) The day ends the moment either condition is met: quota is reached and the timer naturally expires, or quota is reached and all 4 players gather together in the exit zone before time runs out (an optional early end); (6) If quota is not met at day's end, the extra-life penalty triggers instead of ending the run (unless the extra life is already spent). |
| Resources | The shared wallet (spent on bets, earned from wins, drained in small amounts by hits, cut in half as a penalty if a day's quota isn't met); the extra life itself — not a refill, but a one-time pass that lets the run continue past a single failed day, spent the first time quota isn't met; time (the 5-minute daily countdown); a teammate's stunned/carried state, functional as a resource in the sense that it can be "spent" to forcibly remove someone from play for a stretch of time. |
| Conflict | The escalating daily quota under time pressure; the tension between an individual player's temptation to keep pushing a bet and the group's collective risk; the Watched player's altered risk/reward that day; the fact that stopping a friend costs the entire team something too, so there's no purely safe way to intervene. |
| Boundaries | Physically, the game is confined to a single casino room (including its integrated break/safe corner) — there is no other space to go. Conceptually, play is bounded by the day structure: nothing carries over within a day except what's tracked (wallet, lives), and each day is a fresh, time-limited attempt bounded by that day's specific quota and rotation of open games. |
| Outcome | Win: quota is met — either by the timer expiring with quota already hit, or earlier if all 4 players gather in the exit zone once quota is reached — advancing to the next day. Penalty: the timer expires without quota met, and the extra life hasn't been used yet — the wallet is cut to half its current amount started at that failed day, the extra life is now spent, and the run continues into a harder next day. Loss: the timer expires without quota met, and the extra life was already used — contract remains, run ends regardless of how much money is left in the wallet. |


This week's core-loop prototype validates the first-person movement and physics layer that Fowl Play's full sabotage loop will be built on top of — it is not Fowl Play's complete core loop, which centers on betting, sabotage, and exit-zone coordination and is documented separately.

Input: WASD is read through Godot's input map (Input.get_vector) to produce a movement direction relative to the camera's facing; Space triggers a grounded check before applying jump velocity; mouse motion rotates the character's yaw and the camera's pitch (clamped to ±70°) for first-person look.

Action: CharacterBody3D.move_and_slide() applies that input as physical movement across a placeholder floor, with gravity pulling the character down every physics frame.

Feedback: the player sees themselves move through 3D space in first-person view — the same embodied, camera-driven feedback the eventual hit/stun/carry system will depend on — reinforced by an on-screen label confirming which directional keys are currently registered.

Loop: press a key → character moves and the camera view updates → release → movement and the label reset to idle → repeat indefinitely from a fixed start position, with no state carried between presses.

Why this first: Fowl Play's signature systems — hit-to-stun, carry-and-drag, host-authoritative physics sync — all depend on a working, network-ready first-person controller. Per the team's Feasibility Justification, the only reliable synchronous testing window is a single weekly lab block, so movement and physics-sync had to be validated before sabotage mechanics could be layered on top of it.

Jump is included as default first-person controller capability rather than a currently-justified Fowl Play mechanic — the casino room as designed has no verticality — and will need either a stated in-fiction purpose (e.g. stepping over a stool, minor traversal flourish) or explicit removal before the mechanic set is finalized.
